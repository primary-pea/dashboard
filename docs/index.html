    What the model found

    anaemia × anaemia policiesfour headline numbers with their uncertainty · click to open
    The headline is a null result; the interesting part is how much it does and does not rule out.
    Why these four are fixed
    • The model was fitted on anaemia in women 15–49 and the anaemia-cluster policies; it cannot be re-fitted in a browser.
    • The selectors above change the descriptive panels and light up the matching rows in the ladder further down.
    How to read them
    • An interval that crosses zero is a null, not a benefit.
    • A future policy predicting current anaemia means something else moves both.
    • The equivalence test says what size of effect the data rule out, not that the effect is zero.
    • Leave-one-country-out asks whether policy helps forecast a country the model never saw.

        The model, honestly

        anaemia × anaemia policies
        What a row is
        • The main estimate re-fitted with one change to the recipe. Dot = estimate, bar = 95% interval, all in points of anaemia per policy.
        • Hover a row for the sample, and the Benjamini–Hochberg and wild cluster bootstrap p-values where they exist.
        The blocks
        • Other lags — two and five years instead of three
        • Sample and income guards — drop thin countries; income in current US$
        • Lead test — policies three years in the future; a real effect should not show here
        • Country-specific trends — each country allowed its own slope
        • Brackets — first differences, five-year differences, lagged outcome
        • One extra control at a time — malaria, fertility, urban share, HIV, sanitation, employment, deworming
        • Subsamples — shorter windows where an extra control exists
        The two smaller panels — the same dose against each outcome the bar offers, and other ways of counting the dose against anaemia.

        Around the moment a policy arrives

        anaemia
        The set-up
        • Anaemia in the years around an event, relative to the year before it (−1).
        • Two events — a country's first dated anaemia policy · its wheat-flour fortification mandate.
        Three estimators, because countries adopt at different times
        • Two-way fixed effects — the textbook version; can mislead under staggered adoption
        • Two-stage DiD (Gardner) — removes country and year effects first, then compares
        • Local-projections DiD — one clean comparison per horizon
        What to look for — a flat line before the event. If the pre-event years already move, the comparison group is contaminated and the post-event dip cannot be credited to the policy.

        Predict a country the model never saw

        anaemia
        The procedure
        • Leave one country out → fit on the other 48 → predict its within-country deviations → measure the error.
        • Repeat for all 49; compare six models from a flat line to income plus every exposure.
        Reading it
        • If policy carried information, adding it would lower the error. It does not.
        • Left of zero in the strip: the policy term helped that country's forecast. Right: it hurt.
        • The country in focus is outlined; click any dot to focus it.

        Where attention should go

        How the score is built — for each of four indicators (anaemia 15–49, anaemia in pregnancy, underweight, overweight), a flag plus the country's percentile on the latest level:
        0improved over the window
        1started in the worst quarter and did not worsen
        2worsened
        • The four indicator scores are averaged with equal weights.
        • Rank stability — the weights are a judgement call, so they are drawn at random 500 times; the bar is how often the country still lands in the worst ten.
        • The level, change and policies columns follow the filters; hover a row for the four component scores.

        • 7 ways of counting the dose
        • 3 lags — two, three, five years
        • 2 income measures — constant and current US$
        • 2 control sets — with and without context

        Reading it
        • Every specification re-fitted and sorted by its estimate; plum dots are the ones whose interval excludes zero.
        • Use the chips to keep only some recipes; hover a dot for its recipe.
        • If the effect were real and large, the whole curve would sit to one side. It does not.

        Where the clusters come from

        How a record joins a cluster
        • Every GIFNA record carries controlled-vocabulary tokens.
        • Each token is matched, case-insensitively, against the 47 patterns below — never against free text.
        • A record joins every cluster its tokens hit; unmatched tokens are reported, not guessed.
        • The anaemia and fortification clusters also come split by source file — policies, programmes, mechanisms — and the model's dose is the dated policies file only.
        On the cards — countries with at least one active record in 2023, and the mean stock. The chosen cluster is outlined; click a card to switch to it.
